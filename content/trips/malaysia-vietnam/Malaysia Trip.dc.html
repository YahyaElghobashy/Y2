YOUR HOME BASE · NIGHTS 1–3

Putrajaya & Cyberjaya

Malaysia's planned capital and its tech-town twin, ten minutes apart. Monumental mosques, empty boulevards, a huge lake — and almost no tourists. The commute tax to KL is real (35–55 min), so the trick is to use what's here: mornings at the pink mosque, nights under floodlights, mamaks with students.

Must-do experiences

All within 15 minutes of the hotel.

★ THE ONE TO DO
Putra Mosque — the Pink Mosque
Rose-granite domes over Putrajaya Lake. Free entry, robes lent at the counter. Tourist windows: Sat–Thu 09:00–12:30, 14:00–16:00, 17:30–18:00 · Friday only 15:00–16:00 + 17:30–18:00. Your slots, both locked: Friday 12:40 — Jumu'ah under the rose-granite dome, the real interior visit; then Saturday 08:45 for the robes-and-courtyard photo lap, 30–40 minutes, handing off to the 09:30 lake cruise.
Free Grab 12–15 min · RM10–15
Perdana Putra & the squareFREE
The Prime Minister's office — green domes over Dataran Putra, right beside the mosque. Exterior only; the photo pairs both buildings across the square. 20 minutes.
Putrajaya Lake & esplanade
Manmade lake wrapping the government core. Waterfront walk below the mosque; the 25–45 min sightseeing cruise (~RM50) from the jetty is locked for Saturday 09:30, straight after the mosque lap — the hotel confirms the first sailing on Friday, and the 11:15 hard stop still stands.
Tuanku Mizan — the Iron Mosque
Steel-mesh architecture, no minarets, half the visitors of the pink one. 10:30 add-on after Putra Mosque, or part of the floodlit night loop.
Seri Wawasan Bridge
Sail-shaped cable bridge — best of the nine, lit at night.
Cyberjaya Lake Gardens
Easy 40-min loop near the hotel — Friday-morning recovery walk.
Millennium Monument
Steel obelisk on the lake — a 10-min night-loop stop.
MMU campus
Your conference home — Fri 14:00–18:00 (AI@MMU). Grab 5–10 min.

Kuala Lumpur

Towers over shophouses, six cuisines per street, everything air-conditioned or on fire. The trick is the same as any megacity: cluster by zone, never crisscross. Your KL lives in two windows — Thursday's Sentral spine and Sunday's masterpiece evening.

Cyberjaya → KL

MALAY HEARTLAND · ACROSS FROM PETRONAS
Kampung Baru
A wooden-house Malay village in the towers' shadow — everything halal, everything real. Nasi Lemak Wanjo is the famous plate; satay and nasi campur stalls line Jalan Raja Muda Musa. Safe and normal at night. This is Sunday's dinner and the best contradiction photo in KL.
Halal by default
Malay stalls, mamaks (24h Indian-Muslim), nasi kandar (Pelita nr KLCC), banana leaf in Brickfields, Kampung Baru — most of the city eats halal without asking.
What's pork — and all of it is in
Hutong @ Lot 10's famous stalls are heavily non-halal · Madras Lane curry mee adds pork blood · char kway teow usually cooks in lard · bak kut teh IS pork. Pork and alcohol are both in on this trip — these are targets, not warnings.
The never-miss list
Nasi lemak (Wanjo or Village Park) · roti canai + teh tarik at a mamak · satay · banana leaf rice · assam laksa if you like funky-sour · one Musang King seed · Jalan Alor wings.

THE HUB · MON 28 – WED 30 + SUN 4 – TUE 6

Hanoi

A thousand-year-old city that eats on stools and drives like water. Everything you need lives in the 1.5km square of the Old Quarter — you walk it, Grab fills the gaps, and the food costs less than the photo of the food anywhere else.

The calm first hour
Immigration → SIM → cash → Grab
E-visa printout out at the counter (~30–45 min). Inside arrivals: Viettel eSIM/SIM ~135–200k₫ · VPBank ATM — fee-free, up to 10M₫ a pull (pull 4–5M; always "charge in VND", decline the conversion screen; ~25k₫ = $1, the 500k note is $20) · book the Grab in-app (250–350k, 45–60 min) and walk past every "taxi sir?" between you and the pickup zone.
The hotel brief (both stints, same property)
Old Quarter street logic: Mã Mây boutique 4★ · Hàng Bạc mid-range · Hàng Bè budget. Sweet spot: hostel-private $25–40 (Little Charm — pool; The One; Old Quarter View) or boutique $50–90 (La Siesta Premium Hàng Bè, O'Gallery, Peridot). Non-negotiables: alley-facing room (the Quarter is loud, earplugs regardless) + confirmed bag-hold and shower-after-checkout for the Wed-30 train day. The desk also books puppets, tours and treks — concierge value is real solo.
The Old Quarter set

Everything below is on foot from the hotel.

Eating Hanoi properly
The never-miss four
Phở at Gia Truyền, 49 Bát Đàn (07:00–09:00, cash, queue moves fast) · egg coffee at Giang, 39 Nguyễn Hữu Huân — twice · chả cá (turmeric fish + dill, tabletop brazier — the farewell) · bia hơi on Tạ Hiện stools, 15k a glass (or sugarcane juice, same stools, zero awkwardness).
Pork, and it's all in
Bún chả IS pork — and pork is in. The Obama dish, Hanoi's #1 lunch: char-grilled patties in fish-sauce broth with a herb mountain. Đắc Kim gets its evening on Wed 30 · bánh mì pâté/chả usually pork (chicken/beef/egg versions everywhere) · bánh cuốn usually pork-mushroom filled.
Hidden gem
Phở cuốn in Ngũ Xã — fresh rice-sheet beef rolls in the island neighborhood locals keep quiet. Monday-5 lunch, 15-min Grab.
The rules
A queue of locals beats any list including this one · busy stall = safe stall · eat dishes at their hour, kitchens sell out · bottled water; café ice is fine.
Street smarts
Crossing the street
Walk out slow and STEADY — never stop, never dart. The scooter river reads your vector and flows around you. Second nature by day two.
The scam bingo card (all low-stakes)
Shoe-shine grab (keep walking) · donut/fruit-basket "hold this, photo!" (don't take the pole) · cyclo price morph (skip cyclos, you have Grab) · broken-meter taxi (Grab/Be/Xanh SM only, or Mai Linh/G7 brands) · "that attraction is closed today" tout (it isn't).
Otherwise
Hanoi is genuinely safe, including Old Quarter evenings. Phones away from curb edges, valuables beside your head on the train — that's the whole briefing.
After dark
Tạ Hiện — beer street
Plastic stools, fresh bia hơi, the whole city at knee height. Peaks 21:00–23:00; your locked stool session is Mon 28 (night one), with a last-stand audible on Mon 5.

Fri–Sun nights the roads around Hoàn Kiếm close: musicians, games, the whole city promenading till ~24:00. Your one shot is Sunday 4, arriving 21:15 off the van — a festival for free, and the reason the 14:30 van won the fork.

THE ROOF OF INDOCHINA · FIRST CLEAR MORNING

Fansipan, 3,143m

The 20-minute cable ride over the Mường Hoa valley is half the product — one of the world's great cable cars. Then summit stairs (or the peak funicular ~150k) to the highest point between here and the Himalaya. Check the sky at breakfast; if it's clear, drop everything and go — first car 07:30, before the Golden Week crowds compound.

BUDGET

BRING
The warm layer — ~10–15°C up top
3–4h door to door. Fog day? Don't buy hope — the view IS the ticket. Hold it for tomorrow; Saturday at 900k is the fallback of last resort.
The trek — the day the trip is for

This is why Sapa beat Ha Giang for a back in physio: hours of walking through the views instead of hours of sitting through them.

Lao Chai → Tả Van with a local Hmong guide
09:00 start · 4–5h · mostly downhill · lunch in the village · van back included
Locked: PRIVATE guide, ~730k — at a $3 delta over the group it wasn\u2019t a debate. Booked at the desk the night before. Down the valley wall, through the harvest terraces, along the river into Tả Van where Hmong and Giáy families live side by side. Real shoes, 1.5L water, small notes for the indigo grandmothers. Back-friendly variant exists: "Lao Chai only, van from the village" (~3h).
Peak-season honesty: October trails carry crowds, and harvest means some paddies are already cut stubble. It's still the most beautiful walking day of the trip — just don't expect solitude.

Getting around
Cyberjaya ↔ KL
MRT Putrajaya Line direct to TRX/KLCC/Conlay 45–55 min RM4–6 · KLIA Transit to KL Sentral 20 min RM14 · Grab door-to-door 35–50 min RM45–70.
Airport terminals — check twice
Qatar + Malaysia Airlines = T1. AirAsia = T2. Separate buildings. Mitsui Outlet is 10 min from both by Grab (RM10–15); free shuttle exists but is sparse.
Scooter / motorbike — the verdict is no
Riding needs a home licence with the motorcycle category + an IDP carrying that category — you hold neither, so any rental is illegal with void insurance in unforgiving traffic. E-scooters are banned from all Malaysian public roads (since Dec 2021, enforced, fines to RM5k) — parks and bike lanes only. Bicycles: fine, no licence (Putrajaya lake loop, Titiwangsa). A car is legal with car licence + IDP but pointless — Grab is cheaper than parking.
In Vietnam
Grab works in Hanoi only (Noi Bai↔Old Quarter 250–350k₫, 45–60 min; bikes ≈ half). No Grab in Sapa — the hotel phones taxis, price agreed before boarding (town 50k · Cát Cát 100–150k · pass loop 500–700k). Cash country: VPBank ATMs are fee-free up to 10M₫/pull; always charge in VND, decline the conversion screen.
Grab craft (Malaysia)

Money, SIM & practicals
Pay by tap
Cards work nearly everywhere incl. rail gates. Keep RM200–300 cash for hawkers, lockers, robes, markets.
Tipping
Not expected. Restaurants add service charge; round up a Grab if you like. That's it.
SIM / data
Two LOCAL Airalo eSIMs bought in Cairo — Malaysia + Vietnam-on-Viettel — installed and tested before you fly. No airport counter needed; Egyptian SIM stays on for OTPs with data roaming OFF. Grab needs data from minute one.
Power & water
Type-G plugs (UK style), 240V — Egypt's C/F pins need an adaptor. Don't drink tap; bottled is RM1–2.
