YOUR HOME BASE · NIGHTS 1–3

Putrajaya & Cyberjaya

Malaysia's planned capital and its tech-town twin, ten minutes apart. Monumental mosques, empty boulevards, a huge lake — and almost no tourists. The commute tax to KL is real (35–55 min), so the trick is to use what's here: mornings at the pink mosque, nights under floodlights, mamaks with students.

Must-do experiences

All within 15 minutes of the hotel.

★ THE ONE TO DO
Putra Mosque — the Pink Mosque
Rose-granite domes over Putrajaya Lake. Free entry, robes lent at the counter. Tourist windows: Sat–Thu 09:00–12:30, 14:00–16:00, 17:30–18:00 · Friday only 15:00–16:00 + 17:30–18:00. Your slot: Saturday 09:00 at opening (fork #4 — or Bangsar brunch instead, one dies). As a worshipper you can also simply come at prayer times.
Free Grab 12–15 min · RM10–15
Perdana Putra & the squareFREE
The Prime Minister's office — green domes over Dataran Putra, right beside the mosque. Exterior only; the photo pairs both buildings across the square. 20 minutes.
Putrajaya Lake & esplanade
Manmade lake wrapping the government core. Waterfront walk below the mosque; optional 25–45 min sightseeing cruise from the jetty (~RM50) — only if Saturday morning feels roomy, hard stop 11:15 stands.
Tuanku Mizan — the Iron Mosque
Steel-mesh architecture, no minarets, half the visitors of the pink one. 10:30 add-on after Putra Mosque, or part of the floodlit night loop.
Seri Wawasan Bridge
Sail-shaped cable bridge — best of the nine, lit at night.
Cyberjaya Lake Gardens
Easy 40-min loop near the hotel — Friday-morning recovery walk.
Millennium Monument
Steel obelisk on the lake — a 10-min night-loop stop.
MMU campus
Your conference home — Fri 14:00–18:00 (AI@MMU). Grab 5–10 min.
SATURDAY 14:00–20:00 · WITH THE GROUP · VERDICT: KEEP

SplashMania, Gamuda Cove

~25 min from Cyberjaya · conference bus

You're excited about it — that decides it. Group glue, zero logistics, recovery-fun sandwiched between two heavy days. Ride the big slides in hour one while queues are short, then lazy river and wave pool. Afternoon storm cells pause rides briefly and reopen — normal, don't bail early.

BRING

LOCKER

Morning is yours: pink mosque at 09:00 opening (Sat hours), back 11:15 hard stop, lunch with the group 12:15. Waterpark fatigue is real — Sunday starts at 15:00, which is the buffer.
Eating in the base

Cyberjaya is a student town — which means mamaks: 24-hour Indian-Muslim canteens, all halal, absurdly cheap.

The mamak ritualNEVER-MISS
24h · RM2–10 · everywhere
Roti canai dunked in dal, teh tarik pulled from a height, maybe a maggi goreng. Friday night default; the 2am version is the most authentically Malaysian thing you can do. Order: "roti canai satu, teh tarik kurang manis."
Nasi kandar
Rice + ladled mixed curries. Pelita or any busy shop — halal by default.
Student cafés at MMU
Cheap lunch before the 14:00 session — follow the queue.
D'Pulze / Tamarind Sq
Cyberjaya's malls — food courts, air-con, groceries.
Hotel breakfast

Getting to KL & the airport
MRT Putrajaya Line — the sleeper asset
Direct rail from Putrajaya Sentral + two Cyberjaya stations into the city — stops at TRX, Persiaran KLCC, Conlay (Bukit Bintang's east edge) and Titiwangsa. 45–55 min, RM4–6, every 4–8 min till ~23:30. Tap a contactless bank card at the gate. Beats Grab on price 10×, loses ~10 min.
KLIA Transit — airport & KL Sentral
From Putrajaya & Cyberjaya station: 16 min to KLIA (RM9.40) one way, 20 min to KL Sentral (RM14) the other. Every 15 min peak / 30 min off-peak, ~05:00–00:30. This is Thursday's route home from the city and the spine of the whole geography.
Grab — door to door
Cyberjaya ↔ KL city 35–50 min, RM45–70 (tolls included). Station hops RM8–15. Surges in rain and 17:30–19:30. Use rail for the long legs, Grab for the last mile and late nights.

ZONE BY ZONE · 35–55 MIN AWAY

Kuala Lumpur

Towers over shophouses, six cuisines per street, everything air-conditioned or on fire. The trick is the same as any megacity: cluster by zone, never crisscross. Your KL lives in two windows — Thursday's Sentral spine and Sunday's masterpiece evening.

Getting there & around
Cyberjaya → KL
MRT Putrajaya Line direct to TRX / Persiaran KLCC / Conlay, 45–55 min, RM4–6 · KLIA Transit to KL Sentral, 20 min, RM14 (best for the Sentral/Brickfields cluster) · Grab door-to-door 35–50 min, RM45–70. Airport: Ekspres from Sentral 28 min RM55; AirAsia flies from Terminal 2, Qatar & MH from Terminal 1.
In KL: just tap
Your contactless Visa/Mastercard taps directly at every rail gate (MRT, LRT, Monorail, KLIA lines) — no ticket, no top-up. Backup: Touch 'n Go card, RM10 at 7-Eleven. GoKL city buses cost foreigners RM1. City rail hops RM1.30–2.50. The covered air-con walkway Bukit Bintang ↔ KLCC (10 min) is the one long walk that's always pleasant — elsewhere walk mornings/evenings, ride 12:00–16:00. Scooters: see Essentials — the answer is no.

DEEP DIVE · YOUR "NON-NEGOTIABLE"

Jalan Alor

Bukit Bintang · nightly ~17:00–03:00

KL's night food street — a full block of charcoal smoke, plastic stools and shouting. It peaks after 20:00. Your slot: the Sunday nightcap at 22:15, arriving fed from Kampung Baru so you graze instead of committing.

ANCHOR ORDER
Wong Ah Wah grilled chicken wings
THE DARE
Musang King durian at the street's mouth
GOOD TO KNOW
Stalls are mixed — halal and non-halal side by side; satay, grilled seafood and the wings are safe bets, ask per stall if it matters (fork #1). No durian into hotels or trains after — genuinely banned. Prices are honest; you only haggle at markets, not food stalls.

MALAY HEARTLAND · ACROSS FROM PETRONAS
Kampung Baru
A wooden-house Malay village in the towers' shadow — everything halal, everything real. Nasi Lemak Wanjo is the famous plate; satay and nasi campur stalls line Jalan Raja Muda Musa. Safe and normal at night. This is Sunday's dinner and the best contradiction photo in KL.
Halal by default
Malay stalls, mamaks (24h Indian-Muslim), nasi kandar (Pelita nr KLCC), banana leaf in Brickfields, Kampung Baru — most of the city eats halal without asking.
Flags to know (fork #1)
Hutong @ Lot 10's famous stalls are heavily non-halal · Madras Lane curry mee adds pork blood · char kway teow usually cooks in lard · bak kut teh IS pork. If pork's out, skip those; the city still overfeeds you.
The never-miss list
Nasi lemak (Wanjo or Village Park) · roti canai + teh tarik at a mamak · satay · banana leaf rice · assam laksa if you like funky-sour · one Musang King seed · Jalan Alor wings.

Shopping — the mission map
Identical knock-offs → Petaling Street
Sunday 16:00 slot. Open at a third of first price, walk away once, settle near half. Cash.
Tech → Plaza Low Yat
Six floors in Bukit Bintang. Only worth the Sunday 40 minutes if you have a target (fork #2) — accessories haggle, big brands don't.
Outlet brands → Mitsui (Tue 6 Oct)
Nike/Adidas/Puma factory, Coach, Levi's, 30–70% off, 17:45–21:30 window. No fakes, no tech there.
Souvenirs → Central Market + IAMM shop
Batik, pewter, crafts under air-con; the Islamic Arts Museum bookshop is the quality-gift move. Full-price malls (Pavilion, Suria) got no slot — Mitsui covers brands cheaper.
After dark
KL Tower Sky Deck at sunset
Your one deck, rightly chosen — the only one with Petronas IN the frame. Pre-book ~18:30 slot; sunset 19:06; Sky Box after golden hour. ~RM90–200 intl tiers; open deck closes in storms (indoor level still runs).
Saloma Link + Lake Symphony
The lit betel-leaf bridge with Petronas behind — night is its best look. Then KLCC park: fountain shows every ~30 min, 20:00–22:00.
Heli Lounge & Changkat
Drinks on a working helipad, no railings — go 18:30 or as a Sunday 23:00 nightcap if legs remain. Changkat Bukit Bintang is the rowdy bar street. Both optional (fork #1).
River of Life after 19:30
Masjid Jamek confluence lit electric blue + Sultan Abdul Samad floodlit. Lives in Thursday's dinner-skip variant; otherwise a future-trip debt.

Getting around
Cyberjaya ↔ KL
MRT Putrajaya Line direct to TRX/KLCC/Conlay 45–55 min RM4–6 · KLIA Transit to KL Sentral 20 min RM14 · Grab door-to-door 35–50 min RM45–70.
Airport terminals — check twice
Qatar + Malaysia Airlines = T1. AirAsia = T2. Separate buildings. Mitsui Outlet is 10 min from both by Grab (RM10–15); free shuttle exists but is sparse.
Scooter / motorbike — the verdict is no
Riding needs a home licence with the motorcycle category + an IDP carrying that category — you hold neither, so any rental is illegal with void insurance in unforgiving traffic. E-scooters are banned from all Malaysian public roads (since Dec 2021, enforced, fines to RM5k) — parks and bike lanes only. Bicycles: fine, no licence (Putrajaya lake loop, Titiwangsa). A car is legal with car licence + IDP but pointless — Grab is cheaper than parking.
Grab craft
Pickups at marked e-hailing bays in airports/malls · surge in rain + 17:30–19:30 · drop an exact pin, buildings are huge · tolls included · AirAsia Ride sometimes undercuts on airport runs.

Money, SIM & practicals
Pay by tap
Cards work nearly everywhere incl. rail gates. Keep RM200–300 cash for hawkers, lockers, robes, markets.
Tipping
Not expected. Restaurants add service charge; round up a Grab if you like. That's it.
SIM / data
Hotlink or CelcomDigi counter at KLIA arrivals, or an Airalo eSIM before you land. Grab needs data from minute one.
Power & water
Type-G plugs (UK style), 240V — Egypt's C/F pins need an adaptor. Don't drink tap; bottled is RM1–2.
