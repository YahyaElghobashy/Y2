## 4. Night train Hanoi → Lào Cai, Wed 30 Sep — **DECIDED 7 Sep: CHAPA EXPRESS DELUXE, SP7, US$44**

**Why SP7 over SP3:** SP7 dep **22:40 → arr Lào Cai 06:25** vs SP3's 22:00 → 05:55. Forty more minutes of the last Hanoi evening, thirty more minutes' sleep, and zero cost — Botanic doesn't check in until 13:00 either way. SP7 also appears to carry Chapa's flagship suite cabins (suggesting the newer carriage set), though sources conflict — treat as bonus, not rationale.

**Why $44 and not $65:** the SP3 "Premium/Indigo" berth at $65 is the **same 70×180cm bed in the same 4-berth cabin** — +48% for nicer linen and folk-art carriage decor. The $44 Deluxe is Chapa's rack rate and sits inside the $38–46 market band. **The 12go prices were NOT marked up** — they matched Chapa's published list to the dollar. Chapa is simply an expensive operator.

**Lower berth = a REQUEST, not a guarantee** — true on Chapa direct, Baolau and 12go alike; Baolau says so explicitly for private carriages. Decision made knowingly: the upper/lower gamble is accepted in exchange for the experience and the price. **Stack the odds:**
1. WhatsApp Chapa **+84 856 671 990** and get the request on the booking **with a reason** — "lower berth required, back injury under active physiotherapy". A medical reason is handled differently from a preference. Get the reply in writing, screenshot it.
2. Book early — berths come out of a shrinking pool.
3. **The real lever is the cabin.** Lowers are socially negotiable; a polite ask with a genuine reason usually works. Board early from the **Trần Quý Cáp** gate so you're in first.
4. **If you get an upper, plan the two climbs.** Lying flat it's the same bed — the only problem is climbing at 22:40 and 05:45 on a moving train. Use the platform bathroom before boarding, settle yourself and the pack before lights out, don't come down in the night. Two tired climbs become one.
5. Counterpoint worth knowing: in a 4-berth, **lowers double as everyone's seating until lights out**. A lower means company on your bed for the first hour; an upper means you can lie down immediately.

**At booking, confirm three things:** name matches the passport **exactly** (Vietnamese rail tickets are issued against passport details; carry the physical passport to board) · which **carriage class** you're actually allocated (reports of older, harder-bedded coaches going to some passengers) · which train the ticket actually rides, since sources disagree on Chapa's SP3/SP7 cabin split.

**If Chapa is sold out on SP7:** **Sapaly** or **New Livitrans at ~$42** are equivalent products, not a downgrade. Market floor for this berth is ~$38. **Vietnam Railways' own soft sleeper is ~$18 via Baolau** and is the ONLY channel with a real seat map and a genuinely guaranteed lower berth — rejected here on experience grounds, but it remains the fallback if the back flares before departure.
⚠️ **"Orient Express to Sapa" does not exist.** No such operator runs this line — any site selling it is a red flag. Booking channel notes: Baolau is cancellable up to 21 days out; **12go charges 50% once ticketed and refunds nothing inside 72 hours**, and pre-ticks travel insurance at checkout.
